Choosing a retreat company

Executive retreat planners: who does what, and which one you need.

The short answer

Four kinds of companies plan executive retreats: venues, logistics planners, facilitators, and full-service retreat designers that do both the logistics and the room. If the retreat only has to run smoothly, a planner is enough. If it has to change how the team works, hire someone who owns the outcome: the design, the facilitation and the follow-through afterward. Most problems come from a mismatch: a team that needs a real reset hires a logistics planner, gets a beautiful two days, and is back to the same patterns a week later. The planner did the job they were hired for. Nobody was hired to change the team.

Before you sign

Seven questions to ask any executive retreat company.

  1. What outcome will you design to, and how will we know it happened? A good answer names a behavior, not a feeling.
  2. Who will actually be in the room? Many firms sell with a principal and deliver with a subcontractor. Ask for the facilitator's name.
  3. What happens in the 30 days after? If the answer is "a summary deck," expect the effect to fade.
  4. Can you show a measured result from a past retreat? Before-and-after numbers beat testimonials.
  5. What is in the price and what is not? Lodging, meals, activities, transfers, facilitation and travel are often split across invoices.
  6. How do you handle conflict in the room, including when the CEO is part of it? You want a specific answer, not reassurance.
  7. How much of our team's time will planning take? For an executive team, the honest answer should be very little.

An honest trade-off

When a planner alone is the right call.

If your team is healthy, the agenda is already decided and you need a 60-person leadership summit to run without a hitch, a logistics planner or the venue's own events team is the efficient choice. Paying for outcome design you will not use is waste.

Hire a full-service designer when the retreat exists to change something: a team that does not trust each other, a strategy nobody executes, a merger that left two cultures in one room, or a new CEO who needs one team rather than a collection of direct reports.

Straight answers

What does an executive retreat planner do?

It depends on the type. A logistics planner sources the venue, negotiates contracts and runs travel and the schedule. A facilitator runs the sessions. A full-service retreat company like Legendary Retreats does both, designs the whole retreat around one outcome and follows through for 30 days afterward.

How much does an executive retreat planning company cost?

For a full-service, facilitated retreat for an executive team, Legendary Retreats runs $35,000 to $50,000 for a Focused retreat, $45,000 to $150,000 for a Signature retreat and $75,000 to $500,000 or more for a fully bespoke one, exclusive of air travel. Logistics-only planners usually charge a planning fee on top of venue costs, with facilitation priced separately. See what an executive retreat costs.

How far ahead should we book a retreat company?

Three to six months gives you the best choice of venues and dates. A Focused retreat can come together faster because the agenda is ready-built; a bespoke retreat needs the longest runway.

Do we need a planner and a facilitator, or one company for both?

One accountable company is simpler and usually works better, because the venue, the activities and the sessions are designed as one arc. Splitting them works when your team can coordinate the two and the facilitator has a say in the venue and schedule.
