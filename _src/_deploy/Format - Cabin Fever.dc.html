The Cabin Fever retreat.

A remote lodge, close quarters, and no escape hatch. When a leadership team can't slip back to separate hotel rooms and full calendars, the surface politeness wears off — and the real conversations finally happen.

Why it works

Proximity is the intervention.

Most teams manage each other at a comfortable distance — scheduled meetings, polished updates, an exit when it gets uncomfortable. Cabin Fever removes the distance. Shared meals, shared space, long evenings with nowhere to hide. The forced closeness surfaces what's really going on between people, and gives us the room to work with it honestly.

The lodge can be rugged or genuinely beautiful — the constraint is closeness, not discomfort. This is a format, not the product. If Cabin Fever serves your outcome, we run it. If something else serves it better, we'll tell you.

Running Cabin Fever

How it works in practice.

Best for

Teams that are polite on the surface and guarded underneath, or senior teams that have drifted into calendar-only contact.

How long

Three to four days. Closeness needs time to wear the polish off.

Where we run it

A remote lodge or estate, rugged or beautiful, in the mountains, the desert or by water. The constraint is closeness, not discomfort.

Fitness needed

None. This format is about proximity, not physical challenge.

A sample three-day shape

A starting point. Every retreat is built around your one outcome.

Day 1

Arrive

Phones away, one shared space, and the question the retreat is built around.

Day 2

Closeness

Shared meals, shared work and long evenings. Facilitated sessions turn what surfaces into honest conversation.

Day 3

Decide

The conversation that has been avoided, then commitments and one behavior per leader.

Straight answers

What is a Cabin Fever retreat?

A facilitated retreat at a remote lodge where the team shares space, meals and long evenings, so surface politeness wears off and the real conversations happen.

Is Cabin Fever uncomfortable?

Only socially, by design. The lodge can be genuinely beautiful; the intervention is proximity.

Who is it for?

Teams that are guarded with each other, teams that have drifted apart, and teams with a hard conversation they keep postponing.

How much does a Cabin Fever retreat cost?

It runs as a Signature retreat, $45,000 to $150,000, depending on the lodge, length and group size.
