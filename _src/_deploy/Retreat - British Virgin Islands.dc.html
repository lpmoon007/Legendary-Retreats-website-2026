Sailing retreats in the British Virgin Islands.

A crewed boat, a leadership team and a week of passages between islands. Sheltered water with land always in sight makes the BVI the right place for a team to learn to run a boat together, and to talk about what that shows.

Why the BVI

A boat is your company, compressed.

On a boat, nobody can wait out a decision in a meeting. Sails need trimming, a course needs setting, and a crew that does not communicate sees the result immediately. The British Virgin Islands give that lesson the right setting: sheltered water inside the Sir Francis Drake Channel, short passages between anchorages, and evenings at anchor for the conversation the day earned.

Professional captains and crew run the boats and keep everyone safe; your team does the work. Everyone wears a flotation device, and nobody needs to know how to sail or swim. This is a format, not the product. If sailing serves your outcome, the BVI is one of the best places in the world to do it. If not, we will tell you.

What a BVI sailing retreat is good for
Closed-loop communication

A maneuver needs what is happening, who acts, when, and confirmation it landed. Ambiguity shows up at once.

Genuine interdependence

No one can trim, steer and navigate alone. Functions that ignore each other at work cannot here.

Command under pressure

When the wind shifts, the team learns who decides, how fast, and whether everyone follows.

Planning a sailing retreat in the BVI

The practical details.

When to go

December to April is the driest, steadiest and busiest season. The Atlantic hurricane season runs June to November, peaking August to October, and we plan around it.

Getting there

American Airlines flies nonstop from Miami to Beef Island (EIS) on Tortola. Many teams fly to St. Thomas (STT) in the US Virgin Islands and cross by ferry or private water taxi; there are also connections from San Juan (SJU).

How long

Five days aboard is a good length. A full week lets the crew settle and the lessons compound.

Where you stay

Aboard crewed catamarans or yachts, at anchor in a different bay each night, with a night ashore if the outcome calls for it.

A sample five-day passage

A starting point. Every retreat is built around your one outcome.

Day 1

Board in Tortola

Meet the crew, a short first sail to Norman Island, and the framing conversation at anchor.

Day 2

Into the channel

Up the Sir Francis Drake Channel to Virgin Gorda and The Baths. Each day the team takes on more of the boat.

Day 3

The open leg

A longer passage to Anegada, where planning and communication carry real weight.

Day 4

Back west

A sail to Jost Van Dyke and the debrief that turns the week into how the team wants to work.

Day 5

Decide

A morning session at anchor, one behavior per leader, then back to Tortola.

Straight answers

Can you run a sailing retreat for executives in the British Virgin Islands?

Yes. The BVI is one of the places we run multi-day sailing expeditions: crewed boats, sheltered passages between islands, and a facilitated debrief built into every day.

Does anyone on the team need to know how to sail?

No. Professional captains and crew run the boats and keep everyone safe. Your team learns to do the work together, which is the point.

Is a sailing retreat safe?

Yes. Everyone wears a personal flotation device, swimming is not required, and professional captains make every weather call. We plan for weather as part of the experience.

How do we get to the British Virgin Islands?

Fly nonstop from Miami to Beef Island (EIS) on Tortola, or fly to St. Thomas (STT) and cross by ferry or private water taxi. There are also connections from San Juan (SJU).

When is the best time for a sailing retreat in the BVI?

December to April. The Atlantic hurricane season runs June to November, peaking August to October.

Do we need passports?

Yes. The British Virgin Islands is a British Overseas Territory, so every traveler needs a valid passport, including US citizens.

What is included in a BVI sailing retreat?
