Planning a retreat in Colorado

The practical details.

When to go

December to March for snow and winter-survival formats. July to September for high passes and alpine days, with aspen color in mid to late September. Spring and late fall are shoulder seasons, with closed passes in the high country.

Getting there

Denver International (DEN) is the hub. Regional airports put teams closer: Eagle County (EGE) for Vail, Aspen (ASE), Montrose (MTJ) and Durango (DRO) for the San Juans, Telluride (TEX) for private aircraft, and Hayden (HDN) for Steamboat.

How long

Three to four days in the mountains. The Front Range immersion is a single day from Denver.

Altitude

Mountain towns sit between about 6,700 and 9,600 feet. We plan an easy first day and design pacing and hydration around it.

A sample three-day shape

A starting point. Every retreat is built around your one outcome.

Day 1

Arrive and acclimate

Fly into Denver or a mountain airport, transfer up, and an easy first evening at altitude with the framing conversation.

Day 2

The mountain day

A guided day chosen for the outcome: a snow-shelter build or hut traverse in winter, a high pass or via ferrata in summer.

Day 3

Decide

Turn the day into commitments, one behavior per leader, and travel out the same afternoon.

Straight answers

Where are the best places for a corporate retreat in Colorado?

It depends on the outcome. Vail and Beaver Creek pair five-star lodging with backcountry nearby. The San Juan Mountains are the most remote and rugged. The Front Range works for a single day from Denver. Aspen, Telluride and Steamboat Springs fit some outcomes too.

Do you run Colorado retreats for companies based in New York or on the West Coast?

Yes. Most teams fly into Denver International, which has nonstop flights from nearly every major US city, and we handle everything from the airport on. A team can leave the coast in the morning and be in the mountains by evening.

How far are the mountains from Denver?

Vail is about two hours by road, the Front Range high country about an hour, and the San Juans about six hours, which is why teams usually fly into Montrose or Durango for those.

When is the best time for an executive retreat in Colorado?

December to March for snow, July to September for the high country. Mid to late September brings aspen color and fewer crowds.

Is the altitude a problem for executives?

Most mountain towns sit between about 6,700 and 9,600 feet. We plan an easy first day and choose terrain and pacing to suit the team.

Why work with a Denver-based retreat company?

We know the seasons, roads and local partners firsthand, and James leads every retreat himself. Being local also means we will tell you when Colorado is not the right fit.

What is included in a Colorado retreat?

Design, facilitation by James Carter, lodging, meals, activities, on-site coordination and ground transfers, plus a 30-day challenge with a written day-30 report. Airfare is the usual exclusion. Focused and Signature retreats come with a written proposal within 72 hours.

Who a Colorado retreat is for

Private retreats for CEOs and leadership teams of roughly 5 to 12, built around one outcome. We are strongest with teams that need to work as one: teams in conflict, split into camps, or newly merged. Every retreat is turnkey, and Focused and Signature retreats come with a written proposal within 72 hours.
