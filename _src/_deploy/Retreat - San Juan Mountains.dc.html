Planning a retreat in the San Juans

The practical details.

When to go

July to September for high passes, via ferrata and wildflower basins. December to March for snow and winter-survival formats. Spring and late fall are shoulder seasons with closed passes.

Getting there

Montrose (MTJ) is about 45 minutes from Ouray and 1.5 hours from Telluride; Durango (DRO) is about 1 hour 15 minutes from Silverton. Telluride (TEX) handles private aircraft.

How long

Three to four days. The terrain and altitude reward a day to settle in.

Where you stay

Historic mountain towns and private lodges, from refined to deliberately remote, depending on the format.

A sample three-day shape

A starting point. Every retreat is built around your one outcome.

Day 1

Arrive and acclimate

Fly into Montrose or Durango, transfer into the mountains, and an easy first evening at altitude.

Day 2

The range

A guided day chosen for the outcome: a high pass by jeep, a via ferrata, or in winter a snow-shelter build.

Day 3

Decide

Turn the day into commitments, one behavior per leader, and travel out.

Straight answers

When is the best time to run a retreat in the San Juan Mountains?

July to September for the high country, December to March for snow formats. Many passes are closed in spring and late fall.

Which airport serves the San Juan Mountains?

Montrose (MTJ) for Ouray and Telluride, Durango (DRO) for Silverton, and Telluride (TEX) for private aircraft.

Is the altitude a problem for executives?

Towns sit around 7,700 to 9,300 feet. We plan an easy first evening, keep hydration and pacing in the design, and choose terrain to suit the team.

What results have come from the San Juans?

Our largest measured change, a 268% behavioral gain in 30 days, came from a winter-survival retreat in this range.
