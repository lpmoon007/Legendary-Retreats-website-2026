Lake Tahoe · California & Nevada

Executive retreats & offsites at Lake Tahoe.

Alpine water, granite peaks and four seasons of terrain, an hour from Reno and a drive or short flight from the Bay Area. Nature big enough to reset a team, close enough to actually get everyone there.

Why Tahoe

Big enough to reset the team. Close enough to get them there.

Tahoe's draw is range. A leadership team can sit in a five-star lakeside lodge for the hard strategy conversation in the morning and be on the water or the trail by afternoon. It's one of the few places that pairs genuine wilderness with real accessibility, which matters when you're trying to assemble a busy executive team without losing two days to travel.

It fits strategy offsites, leadership-team building, and four-season retreats that want nature without the logistics of the deep backcountry. As always, the place is a means, not the product: if Tahoe serves your outcome, we run it; if somewhere else serves it better, we will tell you.

The lake gives you choices. The North Shore (Incline Village, Truckee, Northstar) is quieter and closer to Truckee’s private airport. The South Shore (Stateline, Heavenly) is livelier, with more evening options. On the West Shore, the Rubicon Trail starts near Tahoma: one of the best-known off-road routes in the country, and a day on it changes how a team talks to each other.

The Rubicon Trail, West Shore. One of the ways a Tahoe retreat gets a team out of the room.

What a Tahoe retreat or offsite is good for
Accessible wilderness

Real mountains and open water without a bush plane. Easy to gather a busy team from the Bay Area or Reno.

Four-season range

Lakeside summers, the Rubicon, alpine trails and winter snow. The same place flexes to the outcome you need.

Strategy + shared adventure

Hard conversations indoors, shared challenge outdoors, in a single trip.

Planning a retreat in Tahoe

The practical details.

When to go

June to September for lake, trail and granite; December to March for snow. Fall is a quiet, beautiful shoulder season.

Getting there

Reno-Tahoe (RNO) is about 1 hour; Sacramento (SMF) about 2 hours. From San Francisco it is roughly a 3.5 to 4.5 hour drive, or under an hour by private aircraft into Truckee (TRK).

How long

Two to three days. Accessibility is Tahoe's advantage.

Where you stay

A five-star lakeside or mountain lodge with real meeting space.

A sample three-day shape

A starting point. Every retreat is built around your one outcome.

Day 1

Arrive

Fly into Reno, an hour to the lake, and dinner on the water.

Day 2

Work and terrain

Strategy in the morning; on the water, the Rubicon or the snow in the afternoon.

Day 3

Decide

A focused morning to land decisions and one behavior per leader.

What it costs

Every retreat here follows our three tiers: Focused $35,000–$50,000, Signature $45,000–$150,000 and Bespoke $75,000 and up, turnkey and exclusive of air travel. What moves the number here: ski-season and summer lodge rates, and private activities on the lake or mountain. See what an executive retreat costs and the three tiers.

Straight answers

When should we hold a Lake Tahoe retreat?

Summer (June to September) or winter (December to March); fall is a quieter, often better-value shoulder season.

How far is Lake Tahoe from the airport?

About an hour from Reno-Tahoe International and about two hours from Sacramento. Truckee-Tahoe airport (TRK) takes private aircraft.

Is Tahoe good for a short offsite?

Yes. Its accessibility makes a two-day offsite realistic for a busy executive team.

Can we combine strategy work and outdoor challenge?

That is Tahoe's strength: a hard conversation in the morning and the water or trail by afternoon.

Can you run a retreat in Lake Tahoe for a leadership team of 8 to 12?

Yes. Retreats in Lake Tahoe are private and designed for leadership teams of roughly 5 to 12. Where you stay: a five-star lakeside or mountain lodge with real meeting space. Larger groups are possible with a different format; we will tell you honestly what changes.

What is included in a retreat in Lake Tahoe?

Design, facilitation by James Carter, lodging, meals, activities, on-site coordination and ground transfers, plus a 30-day challenge with a written day-30 report. Airfare is the usual exclusion. Focused and Signature retreats come with a written proposal within 72 hours.

How far is Lake Tahoe from San Francisco and the Bay Area?

Roughly 3.5 to 4.5 hours by car, longer on winter weekends. By private aircraft, Truckee-Tahoe (TRK) is about an hour from the Bay Area, and 15 to 30 minutes from the North and West Shore. For most teams flying commercially, Reno is the easiest door.

North Shore or South Shore for a corporate retreat?

North Shore (Incline Village, Truckee, Northstar) is quieter and closest to Truckee’s airport, which suits a focused leadership retreat. South Shore (Stateline, Heavenly) is livelier at night. We choose based on the outcome and the group, not the postcard.

Can a Lake Tahoe retreat include the Rubicon Trail?

Yes. The Rubicon Trail runs about 12 miles of unmaintained track between Loon Lake and Lake Tahoe’s West Shore near Tahoma. We run guided off-road days on it in summer and early fall, with experienced drivers and no off-road experience needed from the team.

Can you run a winter retreat at Lake Tahoe?

Yes. December to March, with snow formats such as Winter Survival and Cabin Fever built around the lodge. Skiing at Palisades Tahoe, Northstar or Heavenly is optional; the retreat is built around one outcome.

Who a retreat in Lake Tahoe is for

Private retreats for CEOs and leadership teams of roughly 5 to 12, built around one outcome. We are strongest with teams that need to work as one: teams in conflict, split into camps, or newly merged. Every retreat is turnkey, and Focused and Signature retreats come with a written proposal within 72 hours.
