Retreat ROI · 2026

Is an executive retreat worth the cost?

Sometimes. A retreat is worth it when a specific problem on the leadership team is costing more than the retreat, and when someone measures whether behavior changed afterward. Here is how to tell, with our own numbers.

By James Carter · Updated October 2026

The short answer

  • Worth it: a leadership team of 5 to 12 with one named problem (conflict, camps after a merger, no shared priorities) and a commitment to track behavior for 30 days afterward.
  • Probably worth it: a strategy reset where the team is healthy but needs uninterrupted time to make decisions it keeps deferring.
  • Not worth it: a reward trip with an agenda bolted on, a retreat chosen to avoid a hard personnel decision, or one nobody will measure.

What it actually costs

Turnkey executive retreats for a team of roughly 5 to 12 typically run from about $35,000 for a focused two-to-three-day retreat to $150,000 or more for a multi-day signature retreat, with airfare usually extra. Our own published tiers are Focused $35,000 to $50,000, Signature $45,000 to $150,000 and Bespoke $75,000 and up. The full cost breakdown and the cost estimator show what moves the number.

The cost that matters more is the one you are already paying. Take an illustrative team of eight executives averaging $300,000 in total compensation: that is $2.4 million a year of leadership time. A $75,000 retreat is about 3% of it. If the team spends even a tenth of its time on turf, rework or decisions that do not stick, the dysfunction costs several times the retreat every year. That arithmetic is an illustration, not a study; run it with your own numbers.

What a retreat that pays off has in common

In our experience across 25 years of retreats, the ones that pay for themselves share four things.

  • One outcome, named in advanceThere is only one question that matters: if we accomplish nothing else on this retreat, we will ____. The answer describes the whole leadership team, not two people on it. For example: leave as one team that makes decisions together and carries them out with one voice.
  • Shared experience, not just discussionTrust is earned by doing something hard together. A conference room produces agreement that evaporates by Tuesday.
  • One commitment per leaderA single behavior each person will practice, because one survives a busy week and ten do not.
  • Measurement after the retreatA baseline before, daily tracking for 30 days after, and a written report. Without it, "worth it" is a feeling.

Five signs it is not worth it (yet)

  • The real problem is one person. A retreat will not fix a hire that needs to be exited, and it can make the rest of the team cynical if it is used to avoid the decision.
  • The CEO will not participate as a member of the team. If the leader stays above the experience, the team learns that candor is optional.
  • Nobody can finish the sentence. “If we accomplish nothing else on this retreat, we will ____.” If the team cannot agree on one answer, you are buying an event.
  • There is no time for follow-through. Without 30 days of reinforcement, most retreat insight fades within weeks.
  • You need a plan, not a team. If the team trusts each other and only needs a strategic plan, a focused strategic planning retreat or a facilitator is cheaper.

How to make the case to your CEO or board

Name the problem in the team's own words, estimate what it costs each quarter, answer the one question (if we accomplish nothing else on this retreat, we will ____), and agree in advance what you will measure at day 30. A proposal framed that way is a business decision, not a perk. Our questions to answer before an executive retreat make a good first draft.

Questions buyers ask

Is an executive leadership retreat worth the cost?

It is worth it when a named problem on the leadership team costs more than the retreat and someone measures behavior change afterward. Published Legendary Retreats results range from a 113% to a 268% gain in tracked behaviors at day 30. It is not worth it as a reward trip, to avoid a personnel decision, or when nobody will measure the outcome.

What should an executive retreat accomplish?

One thing, agreed before anyone books a flight. Answer one question as a team: if we accomplish nothing else on this retreat, we will ____. The answer should describe the whole leadership team, such as operating as one team that decides together and executes with one voice, not a fix for one or two people.

How much does an executive retreat cost?

Turnkey retreats for a leadership team of about 5 to 12 typically cost $35,000 to $150,000 or more, airfare usually extra. Legendary Retreats publishes Focused ($35,000 to $50,000), Signature ($45,000 to $150,000) and Bespoke ($75,000 and up) tiers.

How do you measure the ROI of a leadership retreat?

Record a baseline of three to five specific behaviors before the retreat, give each leader one commitment, track it daily for 30 days, and compare against the baseline in a written report at day 30. Business outcomes such as revenue take longer and have more causes, so treat them as supporting evidence.

What is the biggest reason executive retreats fail?

No follow-through. Insight from the retreat fades within weeks unless each leader practices one specific behavior and someone tracks it once the team is back at work.
